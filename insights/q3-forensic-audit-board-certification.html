What This Is

This is human-to-AI intermediation, which I call H2AI: an executive with board and leadership experience working in tandem with multiple frontier models, each doing what the other cannot.

The models bring reach and stamina. They can read every page, cross-check every figure, and argue the opposite case without fatigue. What they lack is context: what this board has been told before, which commitments carry weight with this investor, which leader on your team reads the plan differently and why. That context is the part that decides whether a finding matters. It comes from having done the executive work, presented to boards, and aligned leadership teams that did not start out aligned.

The work has two halves, and both matter.

  1. Take it apart. Whatever you bring, a problem, a scenario, or a deliverable, is examined the way a skeptical audience will examine it. Models from different providers are set against it and against each other, the core premises are inverted to test whether the opposite case is better supported, and where the models disagree, the disagreement is recorded rather than averaged away.
  2. Build it back. Whatever breaks is repaired: claims are sourced, qualified, or removed; figures are tied out across every page; missing commitments are addressed directly rather than left for someone else to find; and the narrative is rewritten so the argument a director hears first is the one the whole deliverable defends.

An audit without the rebuild is only an autopsy. You receive both.

The work is adversarial against the work, the process, and the premise. It is never adversarial against people, and anything it takes apart, it ends by rebuilding into something you can use. The hardening process behind it is one I have spent the past year refining as part of what I call generative governance.
