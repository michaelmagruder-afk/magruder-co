None of these responses answer the audit committee's core governance requirement. The Nondelegable Governance Invariant
A technology vendor terms sheet is not a corporate governance policy. The CEO cannot delegate AI liability to external model providers, junior engineering teams, or functional procurement proxies. You must enter the fall board cycle with an authoritative Governed AI Manifest you can personally defend.

Why Vendor Term Sheets Fail Audit Committees

Most enterprise AI adoption currently proceeds under an illusion of contractual safety. Technology vendors assure buyers that customer data is not used for model training, leading executive teams to believe their governance burden has been resolved.

This assumption fails under regulatory scrutiny for three material reasons:

  1. The Lineage Blind Spot: Foundation model vendors routinely disclaim liability for outputs. If an enterprise deploys an agent that generates defamatory statements, infringes proprietary code, or executes unauthorized transactions, the commercial vendor carries zero liability. The enterprise bears 100 percent of the legal and financial exposure.
  2. Shadow Agent Proliferation: Across business divisions, teams are quietly integrating third-party API keys, extensions, and automated workflows. Without centralized architectural governance, the enterprise has no master registry of where company intellectual property is flowing or what autonomous permissions have been granted.
  3. The Summary Trap: Relying on unverified AI summaries rather than verbatim provenance creates audit breakdowns. When compliance inquiries demand evidentiary proof of how a decision was reached, compressed summaries cannot satisfy legal discovery standards.

The Four Sovereign Pillars of the Governed AI Manifest

The Executive Governed AI Manifest is an immutable, board-ratified corporate instrument. It establishes sovereign operating boundaries before enterprise capital and operational reputation are committed.

Pillar 01
Model Lineage, Intellectual Property, and Training Liability

Every model, fine-tuning dataset, and agent deployment must maintain an unbroken chain of custody. The Manifest establishes strict cryptographic verification for all source training inputs, preventing inherited copyright liabilities and ensuring proprietary trade secrets never leak into external vendor weights.

Pillar 02
Autonomous Agent Decision Rights and Capital Boundaries

Autonomous AI systems must never possess unmonitored execution authority. The Manifest codifies hard programmatic ceilings: financial transaction limits, human-in-the-loop review thresholds, and strict API access controls. No autonomous agent is permitted to execute contracts or mutate financial records without documented executive authorization.

Pillar 03
Multi-Model Tribunal Governance (Sovereign Conclave)

Single-model reliance introduces catastrophic bias and hallucination risk. Under GenGov OS, high-consequence enterprise decisions undergo blinded adjudication across an isolated tribunal of frontier foundation models (Anthropic Claude, OpenAI ChatGPT, Google Gemini Ultra, and Perplexity Pro). The Sovereign Conclave acts as an independent judicial check, stress-testing corporate strategy before external exposure.

Pillar 04
The Evidentiary Living Corpus: Audit Lineage & Memory Preservation

The Manifest mandates that the enterprise preserve complete, human-readable forensic archives of all AI-mediated strategic sessions and customer interactions. Instead of relying on lossy summaries, the enterprise maintains an enduring Living Corpus that satisfies regulatory discovery and preserves corporate memory across executive transitions.
