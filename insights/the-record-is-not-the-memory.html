The Record Is Not the Memory

Beyond RAG and CAG: Governed Corporate Memory and the Zero-Discrepancy EBITDA Architecture for Private Equity
By Michael Magruder
Published October 2026
Authority Magruder & Company Sovereign Canon

Every company deploying commercial artificial intelligence today is building on sand without knowing it.

The work feels solid. A strategy team spends three weeks working with an AI assistant. Together, they build a pricing model, test a software script, or negotiate terms for a new deal. Decisions are made, rules are set, and numbers are locked.

Then the conversation grows too long. The software does what the industry built it to do: it compresses. It summarizes. It keeps the gist.

The Gist Is Where the Silent Damage Happens A strict rule like "do not exceed 4.2 percent margin variance" becomes "keep margins around four percent." An essential system flag drops out of a script. A client rule turns into a casual paraphrase the client never said. Nobody notices right away: the AI repeats the corrupted fact with the identical confidence it gave the true one. This is the quiet failure of modern enterprise AI: systems do not lose data, they lose accuracy, and they never tell you.

1. The Diligence Dilemma: Why Generic Enterprise AI Fails in Private Equity

Large language models have transformed generic knowledge work, but their adoption across Private Equity investment teams and portfolio operations remains stalled by an unforgiving reality: financial diligence, EBITDA bridges, and value creation plans cannot tolerate hallucination, non-deterministic arithmetic, or unvetted data ingestion.

When an advisory firm or deal team analyzes a prospective acquisition, an error of $500,000 in EBITDA normalization does not represent a minor drafting variance. At a prevailing 10x entry multiple, that single discrepancy translates into a $5,000,000 mispricing of enterprise value, corrupts debt covenants, and shatters investment committee credibility.

Standard enterprise AI patterns: specifically naive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and massive Context-Augmented Generation (CAG): fail to resolve this problem. In practice, they amplify risk.

The Three Critical Failure Modes of Naive RAG in Financial Modeling

  1. Context Fragmentation via Vector Chunking: Standard RAG pipelines slice text into arbitrary 500-token chunks. In a trial balance, an account description resides on line 42 while its offsetting contra-revenue deduction resides on line 88. Slicing these into separate vector chunks severs their arithmetic relationship, causing the model to summarize revenue while omitting the offsetting contractual deduction.
  2. The Loss of Alphanumeric Exactitude: Dense embedding models operate in continuous semantic vector space. They excel at identifying conceptual similarity (recognizing that "customer turnover" relates to "client attrition"), but they fail at exact alphanumeric identification. Confusing Account 6100 (Operational Travel) with Account 6110 (One-Time M&A Due Diligence) results in an improper EBITDA add-back that invalidates the normalization schedule.
  3. Absence of Mathematical Governance: Because language models operate via next-token probability rather than an arithmetic execution unit, they cannot guarantee that the sum of line-item add-backs matches the total adjusted EBITDA figure presented to the investment committee.

2. The Architecture: Governed Corporate Memory Substrate

To overcome the fatal flaws of RAG and CAG, Magruder & Company developed the Governed Corporate Memory Substrate.

Rather than allowing an AI model to index uncurated data rooms or scrape messy text files, Corporate Memory operates as an auditable, on-device SQLite datastore containing certified, atomic Solution Units:

Exhibit 1: The Governed Corporate Memory Retrieval Pipeline: Five Sequential Gating Stages from Ingestion to Decision-Ready Deliverables.

Core Architectural Invariants

  • Atomic Solution Units (<600 Tokens): Every entry in the corporate memory bank is a discrete, single-purpose operational or financial pattern, ensuring solutions are modular and verified.
  • The 40% Commercial Floor: To prevent engineers from turning the platform into internal plumbing, the memory bank enforces a binding invariant: at least 40.0% of all stored units must be dedicated to client-facing commercial strategy, PE portfolio operations, pricing, and diligence.
  • 180-Day Epistemic Lifecycle (TTL): Every solution unit carries an observed timestamp and an automatic 180-day expiration clock. If not re-confirmed against real engagements within 180 days, it automatically transitions to quarantined status.
  • On-Demand Context Budgeting (<1,800 Tokens): Traditional CAG dumps hundreds of thousands of tokens into prompts. Governed Memory enforces a strict budget: no query may retrieve more than three Solution Units (under 1,800 tokens total), ensuring downstream models operate with laser focus.

3. The Retrieval Mechanism: Hybrid Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)

Our engine combines SQLite FTS5 BM25 (exact lexical matching) with on-device semantic cosine scoring using standard Reciprocal Rank Fusion with a smoothing constant of k=60:

RRF_Score(doc) = [ 1 / (60 + Rank_BM25(doc)) ] + [ 1 / (60 + Rank_Semantic(doc)) ]

When an analyst searches for an explicit institutional methodology such as SOL-024 or Rule of 40, SQLite FTS5 BM25 assigns it top lexical priority in sub-millisecond time (<1ms). When searching for conversational concepts like "deal economics", the dense semantic branch identifies the EBITDA bridge and VCP decomposition units even if the word "EBITDA" was never typed. The entire search executes locally with zero API calls.

5. Three Questions Every Executive Must Ask Internally Tomorrow

To find out whether your company is exposed to un-governed AI memory drift, ask your technical and operational leaders these three questions tomorrow morning:

  1. To your Chief Information Officer: If an auditor, regulator, or acquisition buyer asks for byte-exact proof of how our AI reached a pricing calculation three months ago, can we produce the original immutable log file, or only an AI summary of it?
  2. To your Enterprise Architecture Team: Where are our historical AI session transcripts stored on disk, and do our active language models have system permissions to edit, compress, or overwrite those past files?
  3. To your General Counsel and Risk Committee: When our autonomous systems cite past corporate decisions to justify current actions, does our software mechanically verify a cryptographic evidence receipt before proceeding, or does it rely on probabilistic recall?

6. The Five Invariants of Governed Financial AI

  1. Never Allow LLMs to Perform Math: Subordinate all quantitative metrics, additions, and subtractions to deterministic code engines.
  2. Never Ingest Unstructured Vector Chunks in Diligence: Store operational knowledge in atomic, curated Solution Units with strict 180-day lifecycles.
  3. Always Enforce Context Budgets: Limit prompt retrieval to at most three units (<1,800 tokens) to ensure maximum reasoning focus.
  4. Mandate Zero-Discrepancy Reconciliation: Require every financial schedule to output an exact $0.00 mathematical reconciliation ledger before rendering.
  5. Enforce Human Maker-Checker Signoff: Terminate all commercial pipelines at local review staging queues, with strictly zero automated external sending.
